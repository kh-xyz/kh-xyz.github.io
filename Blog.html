    15.9.2026. Thoughts on vibe coding


    Vibe coding has became a huge slur on the internet in the past two years. As someone who can program (at the level of minor and something else in computer science), but lacks much of the software developing technology knowledge I have my two cents on this. But what do we even mean by vibe coding? The general definition is, ’’not a single line of human written code’’. But I think this is not a good definition. I wouldn’t call the Lean formalization of the recent Navier-Stokes solution a ’’vibe code’’ even though it was done by thousands of AI-agents.

    First of all, I have managed to make few very useful developing ideas come true in like one few hours, which would’ve taken me days or even weeks back in my freshman year introduction to programming class project. Like my recent vibe coded project which makes you abled to press interactive sound making piano keys to write tabulature for the C-chromatic harmonica. There is not need for this small project to be well coded as long as it functions. So basically here vibecoding just saved me shit ton of time. Since I am also an unemployed bum currently, I have made ’’intelligent’’ job website scrapers so I don’t have to check 20+ websites every morning. These then send them to my email. So should I care in these types of projects about how bloated my code is? I most likely shouldn’t. But ofc there is the other side. The actual vibecoded hellbloat. Like the uncs on X flexing that they made something absolutely horrible without knowing ’’hello world’’ type programming. Especially it is very easy to spot badly made scam websites which basically just look vibecoded from the beginning.

    My third and last point. This is actually inspired from Terrence Tao’s blug post. Which is about mathematical visualization apps. Like he says there, he never had time before the era of vibe coding just to make vizualization apps. This definetely helps people understand math research much better and I see only as a positive, where you save time but also understand these concepts faster.

    11.1.2026. On using AI in studying mathematics


    As of recently large language models have became extremely competent in MSc level mathematical reasoning. Myself as a teacher first started experimenting with it more deeply during fall semester for a complex analysis course to combat cheating. For example I could give it a integral to be solved by residue calculus and even the free versions of Gemini, Chatgpt and Deepseek would solve it near perfectly. Moving forwards I would test it on deeper topics such as basic results on Riemann surfaces, topology and algebra. It would still perform relatively well if you knew when the answer was not quite complete or blatantly wrong. Now in the era of mathematical formalization becoming more mainstream and tools like Aristotle AI being abled to make Lean formalized code I actually see this as the future of the field. By far the most useful tool for me has been using large language models to generate LaTeX code. Even with the most free versions, posting a picture of your hand-drawn commutative diagram will be made to Tikz-cd diagram realtively easily.

    In late spring Google would offer registered university students a year of Gemini pro version which I took. They would later introduce the pro-thinking mode that is actually very competent in mathematics. I have been using it as my main large language model for a while now. I have actually gotten it to improve my studying workflow. Since I am currenly applying to PhD positions, I have had to recall much old theory that I have forgotten. Instead of googling or using other search engines, Gemini can connect my questions in an extremely helpful way. But this is assuming that I am a person who knows when the LLM is wrong. What if a student who has never had motivation to learn higher level mathematics just puts everything into a LLM and calls it a day without critical thinking? Cheating has always been around. Googling, old exams, old model, mathematics stackexhange solutions have always been used when stuck. So it really becomes a question of when is it beneficial for yourself. Certainly universities will have to move on from online submissions to combat blatant cheating.

    One other way of using LLM's to boost your mathematical reasoning is actually feeding your own proofs to them. We all have been using grammer-checkers for years and years. It is completely normalized and not considered cheating. I have noticed that atleast feeding a proof into Gemini pro helps you easily spot your own mistakes. Especially typos as if it wasn't a spell cheker as well. These are usually very minor like small assumption coming out of thin air, but in a larger proof this can be a make or break. Sometimes Gemini will roast my style choices of the argument. But that is for every mathematician to judge what is the context for the amount of rigour needed.
